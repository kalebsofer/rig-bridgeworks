## The scenario

I'm a forward deployed engineer. On the morning of the exercise I take over the BridgeWorks account from a colleague who is moving to another client. The client's steering group meets the next morning, and they expect a status update from Rig before then.

BridgeWorks is a UK social-mobility charity. It has about 45 staff and supports about 3,000 students a year from low-income backgrounds into professional careers. Corporate funders back it and receive impact dashboards.

Why Rig is there: to audit and classify the legacy data, certify the key metrics, reproduce the Power BI numbers independently, and recommend a cleanup, so that the migration to the new CRM is built on data the charity can trust.

### What the data task looks like

I get synthetic CSV extracts shaped like the client's real tables. There are no real people in them. The largest file is about 13 MB and under 200k rows.

The extracts:

- A student records export from the CRM: identifiers, contact details, university, demographics.
- The CRM's current programme stage for each student and programme.
- The lakehouse's nightly copies of that stage table, one per snapshot date.
- An export from a third-party platform the programme team uses.
- An extract of the target data model agreed for the Zoho migration.

The questions are in the spirit of:

- How big is this, and how big should it be?
- Is a proposed cleanup safe? Back the answer with numbers.
- Can I reconstruct what happened over time, and how do I know it's right?
- Should a new data source go into the new CRM yet? Give a go / no-go, with conditions and owners.
- (Stretch) What blocks a clean mapping onto the target model?

Use any tools I like, including AI assistants. We'll ask what I delegated to them and what I checked myself.

### Things worth being fluent in

I don't need to study for these, but they're the tools the task rewards.

SQL over files.

- DuckDB (`SELECT * FROM 'data/file.csv'`) or pandas.
- Window functions (`LAG` / `LEAD`, `ROW_NUMBER`) and `GROUP BY` / `HAVING`.
- Anti-joins.

Working with history.

- The difference between snapshot tables (a full copy per day) and change logs or slowly changing dimensions (SCD type 2: one row per version, with `valid_from` and `valid_to`).
- Turning one into the other, and testing that the result matches the source.

Databricks basics.

- Delta tables, workspaces, scheduled jobs.
- What a nightly full-copy load does to storage and run time.

Messy identity and joins.

- Keys that aren't as unique as they look.
- Joining two systems on email.
- Normalisation, match rates and fan-out.

Data hygiene.

- Mixed date formats.
- Duplicate loads and missing days.
- Free-text fields.

Data protection.

- UK GDPR basics.
- Special-category data, such as ethnicity, religion and health.
- What it means to move personal data into a new system or put it in front of AI tools.

Validation. How I'd convince a sceptical client that a number is right.

### Zoho CRM vocabulary 
Zoho CRM organises data into modules: standard ones such as Contacts and Accounts, plus custom modules (for example Programmes, Enrolments and Sessions). Each record in a module has fields, and records link to each other through lookup fields. Data is usually loaded with the import tool or the API, and tested first in a sandbox. A 10-minute skim of the Zoho CRM docs on modules and lookups is plenty.
